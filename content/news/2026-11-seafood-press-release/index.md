The University of Osaka has announced the launch of **SEAFOOD**, a new quantum–HPC collaborative computing platform developed jointly by the D3 Center and the Center for Quantum Information and Quantum Biology (QIQB).

SEAFOOD connects the **OCTOPUS** supercomputer operated by the D3 Center with superconducting quantum computers operated by QIQB through the open-source quantum cloud platform **OQTOPUS**.

The platform enables quantum and classical computations to be integrated within a single workflow and is scheduled to begin operation in December 2026.

SEAFOOD was developed based on collaborative research led by Susumu Date at the D3 Center and Hiroshi Ueda at QIQB, together with their colleagues. As a proof of concept, the team implemented a quantum-selected configuration interaction (QSCI) workflow combining quantum sampling with large-scale classical computation. In the reported demonstration, a four-hydrogen quantum-chemistry calculation was executed as an integrated workflow between OCTOPUS and QIQB's Urchin quantum computer, reproducing the corresponding classical reference result.

In fiscal year 2027, the D3 Center and QIQB plan to provide trial access to SEAFOOD for up to approximately 10 research teams within The University of Osaka through the D3 Center's proposal-based use program and joint research with QIQB.
